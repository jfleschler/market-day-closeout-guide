Close out a market day while the numbers are fresh.

A simple count sheet helps you see what left the stall and what your cash and card records show.

Start with consistent units

List each product in the unit you sell: bunch, bag, box, jar, or another unit that stays consistent for that row. Record its list price and the quantity packed. Add more stock in a separate column if you restock during the market.

Count what came home

At close, count unsold stock as returned. Record samples, giveaways, spoilage, and other losses separately. Then calculate sold units for each product:

Sold = packed + added − returned − samples or loss

Suppose you packed 10 boxes of tomatoes and returned 3. The count suggests 7 boxes sold. At a $4 list price, that row suggests $28 in sales at list price.

Compare with receipts

  1. Subtract your opening cash float from closing cash to estimate cash sales.
  2. Add card and other payment totals from your processor or sales record.
  3. If those totals include sales tax, subtract the sales tax collected before comparing them with count-based sales.
  4. Write down the difference and investigate while the market day is still fresh.

A difference can reflect discounts, bundles, variable-weight prices, timing, tax entry, or a count mistake. The count estimate is a review aid. Keep original payment and tax records for bookkeeping.

Keep the closeout honest

Money collected after booth and payment fees is not necessarily profit. Product cost, labor, income tax, and other expenses may still be missing. The field sheet is an operational record, not accounting or tax advice.

What to bring to the stall

  • A printed field sheet and pen
  • Starting product counts and list prices
  • A note of opening cash
  • Access to card totals at close
