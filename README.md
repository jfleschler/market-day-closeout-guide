# A simple farmers market closeout

At the end of a market, it is easy to remember how busy the stall felt and hard to remember exactly what sold. A small count sheet gives you a repeatable record without recording every transaction by hand.

**[Download the free printable field sheet (PDF)](docs/market-day-field-sheet.pdf)**. Print page one for the stall; page two explains the steps. No account or email address is required.

## 1. Start with consistent units

Before opening, list each product in the unit you sell: bunch, bag, box, jar, or another consistent unit. Record its list price and quantity packed. If you bring more stock later, record it in the Added column. Keep samples, giveaways, and damaged goods separate from sales.

## 2. Count what came home

At close, count unsold stock as Returned. For each row:

**Units sold = packed + added - returned - samples or loss.**

For example, 10 boxes of tomatoes packed and 3 returned suggests 7 sold. At a $4 list price, that row suggests $28 in sales at list price. If you discounted a box or sold a bundle, write that in Notes. The count estimate will not necessarily match your actual receipt total.

## 3. Compare with receipts

Subtract opening cash from closing cash to estimate cash sales. Add card and other payment totals from your processor or sales records. If those amounts include sales tax, subtract the tax collected before comparing with the count estimate.

A difference is a prompt to review, not an accusation or a profit figure. Discounts, bundles, variable weights, payment timing, tax entry, and counting mistakes can affect it. Keep original payment records for bookkeeping. A market-day closeout is an operational aid, not accounting or tax advice.
